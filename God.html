I have structured the study in two parts. The first establishes what is meant by the sovereignty of God, and how this attribute of the Creator fits into the context of His character. The second explores some of the issues and implications of God's sovereignty, such as how it relates to human free will, man's moral responsibility for sin, and the dual problems of suffering and evil.

Definitions and Distinctions

Wayne Grudem's Systematic Theology identifies three of God's attributes as "attributes of purpose." God's Will "is that attribute of God whereby he approves and determines to bring about every action necessary for the existence and activity of himself and all creation." God's Freedom "is that attribute of God whereby he does whatever he pleases." He is not constrained by anything outside Himself. God's Omnipotence "means that God is able to do all his holy will." Each of these distinct attributes represents one aspect of what we will refer to as God's sovereignty. In other words, His actions are chosen exclusively by Him, nothing and no one can stop them, and He has in Himself the power to accomplish them all.

Another meaningful distinction is that between sovereignty and providence. John Piper, in his book Providence, distinguishes the two by writing, "Sovereignty focuses on God’s right and power to do all that he wills, but in itself, it does not express any design or goal," whereas providence connects His right and power with His good and wise purpose. In other words, providence is "God's purposeful sovereignty." Piper notes that many traditional confessions of faith have held to this definition. For example, Question 18 of the Westminster Larger Catechism (1648) asks, "What are the works of providence?" and declares the following answer: "God’s works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures; ordering them, and all their actions, to his own glory." In this study, we will tend use both terms interchangeably, such that God's sovereignty will almost always be paired with His purpose.
